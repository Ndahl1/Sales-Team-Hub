The 7 steps — tap each one the consultant lands

Tap once = landed clean. Twice = missed or weak (becomes the coaching note). Again to clear.

Qualify before you answer

Ask it"Tell me about the install — any issues I should know about?"
Ask it"What's the price difference from what you quoted?"
Enter both numbers — I'll show the gap and a counter.

This is arithmetic on the gap, not a margin decision. For the number that clears the floor — cost, financing, commissions and tomorrow's open capacity — open Next Day Discount in the price book and give back its manager counter-offer.

Grant"[Name], you've been doing this a long time and I respect your opinion. If you truly feel it's easier than normal and the company would be okay at that price, go take care of her — and tell her thank you for me."
Counter"…what I like most about you is you always want to help the customer. But you've been doing this a long time — if you priced it at $[quote], it's a $[quote] job. I'll work with them and do $[counter]. Go take care of her, and tell her thank you for me."

The brief — five parts, tap what you got

Anything not green before you take the customer — ask for it. Especially #5.

Run the Wheel

1 · Open warm"Mr. and Mrs. ___, this is Nels, the sales manager at [company]. I never let a consultant leave without checking in personally — did we take good care of you today?"
2 · Acknowledge · Empathize · By-Pass"I can absolutely understand that. If I were in your shoes I'd feel the same way. Let's suppose you did make a decision today — which option would you choose?"
Isolate the logical"My job is to inform you so you can make the best decision for your situation. If you don't mind me asking — what questions are you still thinking about?"
50 / 40 / 10"Half is materials and labor. About 40% is what it takes to be in business — the trucks, the training, the people who answer at 2 AM. The last 10% is what we keep if everything goes perfect. Can you see how our pricing is transparent and fair? Should we go ahead?"
Inverted wheel"Every project is Time, People, Company, and System. The System never changes — same equipment. So the only way to cut the price is to shrink the circle. What distinguishing factor lets them charge less for this project?"
Next Availability Install Program"When we have a gap in the install schedule, filling it earns you a discount. What would the number have to be?" (No number → step down $200–300 at a time.)

Once they name a number, check it in Next Day Discount before you agree to it — open slots tomorrow move the floor.

5-year math — go slow, write it down"Over five years: the repairs you'll keep making, plus what the old system overpays in energy, plus the replacement you'll end up buying anyway — against replacing today under full warranty. Can you see how it actually saves you to replace today?"
Like / dislike → suppose"What did you like about option B? What do you think [spouse] would say? Suppose [spouse] is on board — which option?" Then: call them now → swing back tonight → book the second appointment.
Home inspector story"Any system over 10–12 years gets flagged in the inspection, and buyers negotiate ten, twelve thousand off. You're buying the system either way — the only question is whether you get to use it."
4 · The ask — a question, not a statement"Should we go ahead and provide this service for you then?"
Still no — protect & date it"Completely understand — you two talk it over. I'm going to have [consultant] check back with you Thursday evening — does that work? And the quote is locked until then."

Close the loop — 30 seconds, ONE note

How the coaching loop closes

    
The second loop runs on what consultants log, and it has one job: no unsold appointment leaves the week without a documented fight, and no commitment goes ungraded.

    
  


  
  

    
Signing in, PINs and the month box

    
The hub opens on a shared link — no account, no password. Three controls in the header govern everything below them.

    
    
      
  1. Your PIN. Pick your name in the Consultant dropdown; the hub asks for your PIN the first time you open your own touch base or track record. It holds for that browser session. PINs aren't printed anywhere — ask Nels if you've lost yours. They exist so somebody else's honest self-score doesn't get saved under your name on a shared office tablet.
    2. 
      
  2. The month box. One picker drives The Board, The Log, The Team, Where We Land and vs Last Year together. It does not drive the coaching tabs — those follow their own week pickers, so last week's touch bases can be reviewed against this month's numbers.
    3. 
      
  3. The sync dot. Green means the Google Sheet has everything. Red means the hub is working from the copy on this device and will push when the connection returns — keep going, nothing is lost.
    4. 
      
  4. Manager. Press it, enter your PIN, and it turns red and shows your name. That unlocks The Log, Import, Setup and Call Coach, adds the notes boxes to the Monday board, and opens any consultant's form without their PIN. It lasts for the browser session only, which is what you want on a shared machine. The end-of-day team rollup has its own separate PIN so a borrowed laptop can't send it.
    Your PIN also says who you are. Whoever it resolves to gets stamped on every coached call you run and on the debrief that call clears — that is what makes the coverage block on the Monday board mean anything. The old shared PIN still opens the tab, but it has to ask for a name, and it accepts no answer: those calls save unattributed and are counted separately. Individual PINs are assigned on Setup.
    Two levels of access. A manager listed under Full access on Setup opens the whole hub. Everyone else opens Call Coach and nothing else — the numbers, the log, the plan, Setup and the Monday board stay shut, and their button reads Coach rather than Manager. Coaching a call has never needed the revenue, so the people running HA/ZT calls do not have to be handed it.
    A third door: coordinator. A name listed under Coordinators on Setup gets Today, Debrief and Follow-Up with the same rights Nels has on those tabs — the team commitment, any consultant's row without their PIN, deleting a bad debrief — so the day's data keeps getting entered when he is out. Their button reads Coordinator, and every other tab stays shut, Call Coach included.

  1. Call details. Consultant, date (defaults to today), customer or job, evaluator (stamped from your PIN, editable), outcome and quote.
    2. 
      
  2. Score the six steps. Tap 1–5 or N/A, tick the behaviors you actually saw, add a note for the specific moment. The badge on each step and the process score at the top update as you go.
    3.

  4. Save. The recap is filed against the Monday of the week the call ran. Past ride-alongs for the consultant list underneath; tap one to reopen it, or Print / PDF for the signed copy.

Every morning · two minutes · revise as the board changes

    
Nels commits four numbers to the company every morning: jobs sold, sold revenue, Google reviews and Family Plans. This tab is where the consultants commit their share of the same four, so everyone is on the hook for the same day.

    
    
      
  1. Nels first, or not. The team commitment at the top is set with the Manager PIN. The line above it says what the month's plan implies for a day and what it takes to land the month on target from here. Consultants can commit before Nels has — the tab doesn't wait.

  6. Read the tiles. Target, days logged, days remaining, revenue to date, and the daily revenue needed to land on target.
    7. 
      
  7. Read the scenarios. Base case runs the current close rate and average sale forward across the remaining days. Best case adds 20% to the close rate (capped at 70%) and 5% to the ticket; worst case takes 15% off the close rate and 5% off the ticket.
    8. 
    

    

      Read it honestly
      
The projection multiplies appointments-per-logged-day by the days remaining. Early in a month two logged days is a thin base and the numbers swing hard; it settles by the second week. Assumptions save per month, so setting September's target doesn't disturb August's history.

Six doors into the Price Book app, and a short table of which tool belongs to which moment in the call.

    

      Why it is links and not a copy
      
The price book recalculates from a cost catalog and margin rules that change. A copy of it inside this hub would be right on the day it was pasted and quietly wrong afterwards — and the person who finds out is a consultant at a kitchen table. So this tab holds no prices, no packages and no cost figures. It opens the live app instead.

      What the lock is and isn't
      
The tool lives at its own address inside the price book app. Hiding the card here hides the door, not the room — anyone who has the URL can still open it. It takes the job type, tonnage, package, lead type, payment method and how many install slots are open tomorrow, and returns the net profit on a proposed price, whether it clears the floor, and the lowest counter that still does. Call Coach links to it from both places a discount comes up — the High Authority quote box and the “costs too much” play.

When it looks wrong

    

      
What you seeWhat it means

      
Sync errorThe hub is working from the copy on this device and will push when the connection is back. Keep going — nothing is lost. If it stays red all day, tell Nels.

      
The Board all dashesNo days logged for that month yet. It fills in as the month goes.

      
No focus card on DebriefNo touch base submitted this week. Debriefs still save and still count — you just won't be asked the focus question.

      
Numbers look offCheck the month box in the header first. Then tell Nels which day looks wrong — he can correct any single day and everything recalculates.

      
PIN not acceptedAsk Nels. Don't use somebody else's — the entry saves under whoever's name is in the dropdown.

      
Close rate above 100%Sold exceeds appointments on some day. The day editor warns as you type; open that consultant's days and find it.

      
Ticket looks wildRevenue logged against zero sold, or a day entered twice. Check the day cards for a duplicate figure.

      
A consultant reads zeroTheir name on the imported report didn't match the roster. Add them on the Team tab and re-import that day.
